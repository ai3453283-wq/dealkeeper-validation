DealKeeper Validation Protocol

DealKeeper is testing two related but separate questions: can an independent service reliably detect when promotional wireless bill credits do not match what a U.S. customer was promised, and do people understand and value that protection enough to pay for it?

Track A — First 30 real-bill audits

  • U.S. AT&T, Verizon or T-Mobile customer
  • Active device promotion or trade-in promotion
  • At least one current bill
  • Financial values entered from the real promotion and bill
  • No carrier login, phone number, account number, contract/order ID or other direct identifier is needed

Real Deal records are marked data_mode=real and eligible_for_first30=true. These are the only records used to evaluate real discrepancy prevalence, reconciliation accuracy and real purchase intent.

Track B — Synthetic product demo

Anyone may try DealKeeper without having an active promotion. In Demo mode every scenario value may be fictional. Demo records are marked data_mode=demo, synthetic_data=true and eligible_for_first30=false. They are never mixed with real-bill evidence.

The synthetic track measures whether people understand the product, can see the value of monitoring a long-running promotion, and would hypothetically consider paying for a service like DealKeeper if they had a qualifying deal.

What we measure

  • Can real customers identify the original promised value and actual bill credits?
  • Does the simple schedule match the real bill?
  • How often do real cases require catch-up/grace-period logic?
  • How many material discrepancies appear in the clean real-bill cohort?
  • Would a real customer pay $7.99, $12.99 or $19.99/year to monitor the remaining promotional value?
  • Can non-eligible/demo users understand the value proposition without sharing real bill data?
  • Would demo users hypothetically consider the same price points if they had such a deal?

Privacy and data-quality gates

The research build does not request carrier credentials or direct identifiers. Optional bill PDFs remain on-device. Real records carry data-quality fields and synthetic demo records are structurally excluded from the First 30 cohort.

Internal gates

The project should not move to a production MVP unless real-user evidence supports reliable reconciliation, low false-alert rates and credible willingness to pay. Synthetic demo interest can support product-comprehension decisions, but cannot substitute for real-bill evidence.
